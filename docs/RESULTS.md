# Results: do the recovered rules predict winters they never saw?

Generated by `python3 -I scripts/baselines.py` from `data/derived/lift_days.csv`; raw numbers in `results/baselines.json`.

**Method.** Leave-one-winter-out: each rule is fitted on seven winters and scored on the eighth, rotated through all eight. Pooled figures add the counts across the eight test winters. Precision = of the calls "will hold", how many held; recall = of the holds, how many were called; F1 combines them. Day level asks: did any lift hold for wind that day? Lift level asks: did this expected lift hold or close that day (any stated cause, including a whole-mountain closure)?

Only 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25 have enough expected lift-days to mean much; the other winters score under 60 lift-days each because most of their posts give a count ("32 lifts scheduled") rather than names.

**Coverage.** Lift-level rules are fitted on the 1,091 of 1,636 side-days (67%) whose schedule post names the lifts, mostly 2021-22 to 2023-24. The side-days left out are windier: 59% have a crest gust of 60 mph or more, against 41% of the named ones. A sensitivity run that infers rosters where a stated lift count allows it (`docs/SENSITIVITY.md`) is data-limited and moves the pooled scores by about 0.01.

**Morning versus full-day wind.** The day-level line above uses the strongest gust of the whole day (06:00 to 16:00), which includes hours after a hold. Using only the gust known by 9am (05:00 to 09:00) gives a pooled F1 of 0.761 against 0.812 for the full day, with a learned line of 47.2 to 55.4 mph. The full-day numbers describe a day; the morning numbers are what a call at opening could have used.

**Simple rules against per-lift rules at lift level.**
- One learned wind line scores a pooled F1 of 0.547, against 0.58 for per-lift rules (any pass) and 0.576 for per-lift rules at 50% odds: fitting each lift separately adds +0.033 of F1.
- The 25 mph rule has the highest recall (0.981) but very low precision (0.229), so its pooled F1 is the lowest (0.371). It tops the F1 table in 3 of 7 winters, but those are the tiny ones.
- Per-lift rules called at 50% odds have the best precision of the learned methods, at the cost of recall: use them if a false alarm is costlier than a miss.

## Limits

- The outcome is the ops account's posts; a hold nobody posted is invisible, and a post parsed wrongly is a wrong label (about 90% of posts right on a 240-post hand check, plus or minus 5 points).
- Per-lift results rest mainly on three winters (2021-22 to 2023-24) that list lifts by name on most days.
- Wind features are crest sensors (Siberia, Alpine Summit); the Funitel-top wind is partly an estimate, and the avalanche and new-snow fixes are described in DATA_SOURCES.md.
- No rule here is a forecast of tomorrow: day-level inputs use that day's own maximum gust.
