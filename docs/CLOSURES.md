# What counts as a closure

Generated by `python3 -I scripts/closure_conditions.py` from `data/derived/lift_days.csv`. Every number is counted from the posts and the weather files; nothing here is a fitted rule. Label: **derived** unless marked otherwise.

## Definition used in this project

- A **whole-mountain closure** is a post that closes every lift on a side or on both sides, with no lift named: "Palisades Tahoe is CLOSED today", "ALL LIFTS CLOSED TODAY", "all lifts at Alpine are closed for the remainder of the day", "will not have any lift operations today" (parser rule P-022, plus rule P-021 for "all lifts ... closed").
- Its scope is one of: both sides, the Palisades side only, or the Alpine side only. The Base to Base Gondola counts as closed only when both sides are.
- It is **not** a closure when: lifts are closed one by one, only the lifts already on hold are closed (that is scope ALL_ON_HOLD), a road, parking lot, tube park or trail is closed, a lift runs on limited operations (download only, mid-station only), or the closure is announced for a later day (that is a warning).
- Time matters. The post time is kept (`closed_all_time`). A closure posted before 10am usually means the mountain never opened; one posted in the afternoon means lifts ran and then stopped.
- The two sides do not always share a schedule. Palisades and Alpine can open and close on different days (Alpine often closes earlier in spring, Palisades can run into May or July 4th in big years), and the Base to Base Gondola keeps its own seasonal timeline. Season windows are kept per side in `rules/season_windows.json`; the gondola counts as in season when either side is. The split-schedule point is a general summary the owner pasted (unverified); the per-year dates in the season file are the evidence.

**Storm** means the posts name wind, snow, avalanche danger, ice, low visibility or "weather conditions". **Lightning** closures happen mostly after noon and mostly in spring. **Schedule** closures are "Alpine is closed today"-style posts in April to June with no cause; they are operations, not conditions. **Cause not stated** is a closure post that names no cause; the conditions columns show what the weather was.

## Limits

- Counts are small (a few dozen closure days), so shares move by several points with one day.
- The kind comes from words in the post, which can name several causes; the tags are post-level, not sentence-level.
- A closure is only as complete as the posts. The parser's closure rule was checked by hand on held-out posts (see `docs/PARSING_RULES.md`): it is the weakest part of the parser.
- Avalanche danger is the Sierra Avalanche Center's forecast rating for the day; it is not the resort's own decision rule, which is unpublished.

## How sure we are that each closure is real

Each closure day is graded by two independent checks: whether the two blind AI readers (Subquadratic, Meta Muse) also call the post a closure, and whether the weather or the named cause makes a closure plausible (gusts of 60+, avalanche danger 3+, 1+ in of snow water, or lightning, power, flooding, visibility, ice or snow named). High = both readers and plausible. Medium = one of the two. Low = neither.

- High 47, medium 5, low 0 (of 52 closure days). `closure_conf` is a column in `lift_days.csv` so any analysis can drop the weaker ones.
- This checks the closures the parser found. It does not find closures the parser missed; the hand check found closure scope errors and a few missed closures (see `docs/PARSING_RULES.md`).

## After a hold: did it reopen?

A hold or closure counts as **reopened** only when a post says the lift (or its group) is open again later the same day. When no reopening is posted the end of the hold is **unknown**; nothing is assumed (decision 63, which replaced the earlier assumption that no post meant no reopening). `reopen_posted` is 1 only when a post says so.

| Crest gust | Hold or closure lift-days | Reopening posted | Median minutes to the posted reopening |
|---|---|---|---|
| under 40 | 535 | 49% | 46 |
| 40 to 60 | 641 | 49% | 52 |
| 60 to 80 | 1163 | 38% | 57 |
| 80 to 100 | 1121 | 24% | 64 |
| 100 or more | 2017 | 17% | 44 |

How to read it: the harder the wind, the less often a reopening is posted, and when one is posted it comes later. Under 40 mph about four in ten holds have a posted reopening, so some of the "assumed none" holds there were probably short holds whose end went unposted; at 100 mph and above the assumption is much safer.
