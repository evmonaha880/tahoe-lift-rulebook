The Liftie Sutras

Send It or End It

The rules that decide when a ski lift stops were never written down. We dug them up.

1

A morning you do not control

It is a powder day. You are in a line that is not moving. The Tram and the Funitel, the two big lifts the rest of the mountain hangs from, have stopped.

No sign says why. No one says for how long. You stand there, cold, while the day drains away.

2

Rules nobody wrote down

Every system has rules like this. In video games, players dig the hidden rules out of the code. That is easy, because the code exists.

The rules that run real life, like when a lift opens, who gets in, when it stops, are not in any code. The only way to learn them is to stand in the line.

3

So we dug

The operators leave traces every morning. They post it: “The Funitel is on wind hold.” We treated those posts like old tablets, and the weather like a carbon date.

Each sutra was tested on winters it never saw. People guess lifts stop around 25 mph. The data says about 60.

Eight winters, eight layers

The operators never wrote the rules down, but every morning they left traces. Each layer is a winter. The number on a layer is how well the book called that winter's wind-hold days when it was hidden, from 0 to 1. Click a layer to read what was found in it.

Hide a winter. Predict it.

For each winter, the rules were rebuilt from the other seven and then asked to call the hidden one. The first chart is the main finding. The lift-level numbers below it come from the repository's own results (scripts/baselines.py), counted on scheduled lift-days only.

Nobody graded their own homework

Our parser read every post. Two independent AI readers, Subquadratic (all 7,044 posts) and Meta Muse (7,040; four calls failed), read them blind. Posts where both readers agreed with each other and the parser differed were pulled for review.
