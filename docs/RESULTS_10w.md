# Results: do the recovered rules predict winters they never saw?

Generated by `python3 -I scripts/baselines.py` from `data/derived/lift_days_11w.csv`; raw numbers in `results/baselines_10w.json`.

**View: 10 winters (2016-17 to 2025-26; no estimated wind).**

**Method.** Leave-one-winter-out: each rule is fitted on the other nine winters and scored on the one left out, rotated through all ten. Pooled figures add the counts across the ten test winters. Precision = of the calls "will hold", how many held; recall = of the holds, how many were called; F1 combines them. Day level asks: did any lift hold for wind that day? Lift level asks: did this expected lift hold or close that day (any stated cause, including a whole-mountain closure)?

Only 2016-17, 2018-19, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25 have enough expected lift-days to mean much; the other winters score under 60 lift-days each because most of their posts give a count ("32 lifts scheduled") rather than names.

**Coverage.** Lift-level rules are fitted on the 1,583 of 2,029 side-days (78%) whose schedule post names the lifts, mostly 2021-22 to 2023-24. The side-days left out are windier: 45% have a crest gust of 60 mph or more, against 49% of the named ones. A sensitivity run that infers rosters where a stated lift count allows it (`docs/SENSITIVITY.md`) is data-limited and moves the pooled scores by about 0.01.

**Morning versus full-day wind.** The day-level line above uses the strongest gust of the whole day (06:00 to 16:00), which includes hours after a hold. Using only the gust known by 9am (05:00 to 09:00) gives a pooled F1 of 0.774 against 0.817 for the full day, with a learned line of 44.2 to 47.6 mph. The full-day numbers describe a day; the morning numbers are what a call at opening could have used.

**Simple rules against per-lift rules at lift level** (same-day features: the whole-day gust is hindsight; the by-9am version is further down):
- One learned wind line scores a pooled F1 of 0.636, against 0.68 for per-lift rules (any pass) and 0.679 for per-lift rules at 50% odds: fitting each lift separately adds +0.044 of F1.
- The 25 mph rule has the highest recall (0.985) but very low precision (0.336), so its pooled F1 is the lowest (0.501). It tops the F1 table in 2 of 9 winters, but those are the tiny ones.
- Per-lift rules called at 50% odds have the best precision of the learned methods, at the cost of recall: use them if a false alarm is costlier than a miss.

## Lift level, using only what is known by 9am

The lift-level tables above let every rule read the whole day's gust (`day_gust`, hindsight: it includes hours after a hold begins) and each lift's own rule may pick other same-day features. This table keeps the same lift-days but lets every method read only what is known by about 9am: the 05:00-09:00 ridge gust, early wind speed, avalanche rating, base temperature and snowfall. It is the fair test of a morning call, and the number to quote if the claim is "predicts the morning of".

## Limits

- The outcome is the ops account's posts; a hold nobody posted is invisible, and a post parsed wrongly is a wrong label (about 90% of posts right on a 240-post hand check, plus or minus 5 points).
- Per-lift results rest mainly on three winters (2021-22 to 2023-24) that list lifts by name on most days.
- Wind features are crest sensors (Siberia, Alpine Summit); the Funitel-top wind is partly an estimate, and the avalanche and new-snow fixes are described in DATA_SOURCES.md.
- No rule here is a forecast of tomorrow: day-level inputs use that day's own maximum gust.
